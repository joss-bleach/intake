## What it shows

Journal screen with a solid warm-orange background filling the top ~55% of the screen, transitioning to a
white sheet below. Top-left: bold white "Eaten" figure ("360" in the logged-meal state, "1482" in an
empty/full-day state) with grey-white "Eaten" caption underneath. Centred: a circular ring gauge (thin
white track, thin gold/cream progress arc) with centred bold white number and "Cal" / "Left" stacked
caption inside (e.g. "2412 / Cal / Left"). Top-right: bold white number with "Burned" caption, mirroring
the left column.

Below the header numbers, a solid dark-navy full-width pill button, centred bold white text "Daily
assessment" — a single prominent CTA sitting directly under the calorie ring.

Main white rounded card (rounded top corners only, floating over the orange field): "Macronutrients" bold
centred title, then four small ring-gauge badges in a row, each a thin coloured ring (yellow/Fat,
pink-red/Protein, sky-blue/Carbs, brown/Fiber) with a bold number-over-"/goal g" pair inside and a label
underneath ("Fat", "Protein", "Carbs", "Fiber"). Below that, "Rating of my day" bold centred title, then a
row with a flat orange circular emoji-face icon (neutral/slightly-concerned mouth) beside two lines of
dark-navy body text: "Careful, your day is orange, there are too many foods to limit" — a plain-language
day-quality verdict rather than a numeric score.

A small chevron toggle (collapse/expand caret) sits centred below the card, presumably to collapse this
summary panel. Below the fold, a pill-shaped date navigator — left/right chevron arrow buttons flanking a
white rounded-full pill with a calendar icon and "Today" label — then the meal log begins: a "Breakfast"
row with a small emoji-face icon, bold "Breakfast" label, grey "360 / 831 Cal" subtext, and the specific
food entry below it in dark-navy text ("Beef-Stuffed Peppers (360 Cal)").

Bottom nav: plain white bar, three items — Coach, Journal (active, bold+dark icon), Profile — house,
notebook/journal, and person icons.

## Why it's here

The colour-graded "Rating of my day" verdict (an emoji face plus a plain-language sentence naming the
colour, e.g. "your day is orange") is a distinctive alternative to a numeric health score — worth
considering for Intake as a daily-summary moment. The four-ring macro badges (Fat/Protein/Carbs/Fiber, each
its own small ring rather than a shared bar) is a compact, colour-coded way to show multiple nutrient goals
at once.
